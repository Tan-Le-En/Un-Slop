# Research foundations

Contents: 1 method and limits, 2 findings, 3 contested points, 4 bibliography.

## 1. Method and limits
Built from web research performed while creating this skill, plus widely established design and accessibility standards. Limits to state honestly:
- Much AI-slop design commentary comes from blogs, vendor sites and developer posts. It agrees on patterns but is largely observational, not controlled study.
- The writing evidence is stronger: Wikipedia's volunteer editors catalogued patterns across thousands of AI drafts.
- Items in section 4 marked [retrieved] were seen in this session. Items marked [canonical] are well-known works the skill relies on from background knowledge; they were not re-opened and should be checked before quoting.
- Several popular statistics (e.g. precise attention percentages) circulate without clear sourcing and are not used here as rules.

## 2. Findings that drive the skill
1. Slop is regression to the mean. Models output the most likely choice; where no choice was made, defaults appear. (Wikipedia guide; design commentary.)
2. Writing tells cluster: significance puffery, trailing analysis clauses, negative parallelism, rule of three, stock vocabulary, summary-everything, formatting overkill. One sign proves nothing; density and missing specifics matter.
3. Fixing tells without fixing substance hides the problem. Wikipedia editors warn against treating the signs themselves as the problems.
4. Detection is unreliable; people who use LLMs heavily identify AI text much better than casual users, but errors are common. Do not optimise for detectors.
5. Design tells cluster: Inter or default sans, indigo-purple gradients, centred hero, pill badge, three rounded icon cards, soft shadows, glass, emoji icons, invented testimonials, "Most Popular" pricing card.
6. Banning the obvious tells yields a second wave of defaults (cream with terracotta, near-black with an acid accent). Choices must come from the brief.
7. Accessibility is an objective floor: WCAG 2.2 AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and for UI components and graphical objects; 44px touch targets are the stricter recommended size.
8. Data graphics: maximise data-ink, remove chartjunk, label directly, do not rely on colour alone, use colour-blind-safe palettes.
9. Reading behaviour: scanning patterns documented by Nielsen Norman Group; hierarchy decides what gets seen.
10. Critique-and-fix loops (generate, name the tells, regenerate specific parts with constraints) outperform one clever first prompt.

## 3. Contested or taste-based
Serif revival, brutalism, bento grids, dark mode, glassmorphism and grain textures are trends with fans and critics. Judge by fit and execution. Minimalism is not inherently better.
