# Design psychology to practical rules

Contents: 1 Gestalt, 2 hierarchy and attention, 3 cognitive load, 4 colour, 5 credibility, 6 reading patterns.

## 1. Gestalt
- Proximity: things close together are read as a group. Rule: gaps inside groups smaller than gaps between groups, consistently.
- Similarity: same look means same role. Rule: one style per role; do not style unlike things alike.
- Continuity and alignment: eyes follow lines and edges. Rule: share alignment axes.
- Closure and figure-ground: viewers complete shapes and separate subject from background. Rule: keep the subject clear against its ground.
- Common region: enclosure groups. Rule: use boxes sparingly; space often does the job.

## 2. Hierarchy and attention
Attention goes to contrast: size, weight, colour, isolation, position. Use few strong contrasts. If everything is emphasised nothing is. Test by blur or squint. Make the primary action unmistakable.

## 3. Cognitive load
Working memory is small. Reduce choices, chunk information, show progressive detail, use recognisable patterns, keep labels consistent. Remove anything that makes the reader decode rather than read.

## 4. Colour
Colour associations vary by culture and context; treat them as hypotheses, not laws. Use colour for function first (state, category, emphasis). Warm and saturated colours advance; cool and desaturated recede. Limit simultaneous hues. Check contrast and colour-blind safety.

## 5. Perceived quality and credibility
People judge credibility fast from visual polish: consistency, legibility, real content, absence of errors. Polish that hides weak content, or fake proof, damages trust once noticed. Honest specifics read as more credible than superlatives.

## 6. Reading patterns
Nielsen Norman Group eye-tracking research documents several scanning patterns (F-pattern, layer-cake, spotted, commitment). Design for scanning: meaningful headings, front-loaded paragraphs, visible structure. Treat popular numerical claims about attention (specific milliseconds, percentages) cautiously unless sourced to the original study.
