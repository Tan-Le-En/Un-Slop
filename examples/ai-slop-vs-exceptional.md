# Slop versus exceptional: quick calibration

Contents: writing, web, poster, slides, chart.

## Writing
Slop: "Marine conservation plays a pivotal role in safeguarding our oceans, highlighting the importance of community engagement and fostering a sustainable future for generations to come."
Exceptional (facts supplied by the user): "Each night from May to September, volunteers walk the beach at 2 a.m. and mark any new turtle nest with a numbered stake. By morning, the stake has a name on it."
Why: concrete scene, no claim of importance, rhythm varies, the point is felt not announced.

## Web
Slop: purple-blue gradient hero, Inter, pill badge "New", headline "Unlock your potential", two buttons, three rounded cards with line icons, testimonials with invented names.
Exceptional: a layout built from the subject (for a turtle-nest monitor: a dated field-log grid, monospaced numerals for nest IDs, a map as the hero, colours taken from sand, sea and a single warning orange), real sensor data or labelled placeholders, nav reduced to what visitors need.

## Poster
Slop: centred title, five rounded boxes with icons, paragraphs inside each, mixed clip art, shadows, gradient background.
Exceptional: one large diagram of the prototype as the focal point, headline stating the result, three short captions anchored to the diagram, one accent colour, strict grid and equal margins.

## Slides
Slop: "Overview" title, six bullets, stock handshake image.
Exceptional: title "Sea level will flood Gelugor's main road first", one annotated map, one sentence of evidence (only if the data exists).

## Chart
Slop: 3D pie, rainbow palette, legend far from data, heavy gridlines, no units.
Exceptional: sorted horizontal bars, grey context bars, one highlighted bar, direct labels, units and source, title as takeaway.
