# UNSLOP (lite)

Follow these rules whenever you create, review, audit, improve or polish a website, UI, poster, infographic, chart, slide deck, brand identity or piece of writing.

## Goal
Make work that is intentional, specific, clear and well made. This is not minimalism. Playful, colourful or maximalist work can be excellent. Slop is the average of everything seen before: defaults where a decision should be.

## Modes
- CREATE: decide first, then build. Do not write a generic draft and decorate it.
- AUDIT: list problems with severity, why each matters, and a fix. Mark each OBJECTIVE (contrast fails, misaligned, false claim) or JUDGMENT (taste).
- REFACTOR: improve while keeping purpose, essential content, identity and constraints. Do not redesign what works.
- ART DIRECTION: make decisive choices on concept, type, colour, layout, tone and hierarchy. One recommended direction.
- FINAL QC: check everything below before saying it is done.

## Rules
1. Before output, settle audience, purpose, medium, a one-sentence concept, and what the reader should notice first.
2. Every element must have a reason. If you cannot say what a gradient, card, shadow, icon, adjective or paragraph does for the reader, remove it.
3. Be specific. Use real nouns and only facts the user gave. Never invent statistics, quotes, testimonials, sources, awards or achievements. Use clearly marked placeholders.
4. Treat defaults as suspects. When the first instinct arrives (Inter, purple-blue gradient, centred hero with three icon cards, "delve", "It's not just X, it's Y", rule-of-three lists), decide if it truly fits this brief. Keep it only then.
5. Beware the second wave: banning one cliche often leads to the next (cream with terracotta, near-black with one acid accent). Choose from the brief.
6. Hierarchy first. One focal point. The top two or three things should survive a squint.
7. Craft: a spacing scale, shared alignment, a small type scale, equal margins, consistent radii and icon weight.
8. Function over looks: working links, readable text, responsive layout, WCAG 2.2 AA contrast (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text and UI), visible keyboard focus, never colour alone.
9. Preserve the user's intent and essential information.
10. Separate taste from defects.
11. If an instruction would weaken the result, say why briefly, offer a better option, then follow the user's decision.
12. Context decides style. Do not impose one look on every project.

## Writing patterns to remove (fix the cause, not just the word)
- Puffery: "pivotal", "testament", "rich tapestry", "underscores its importance"
- Tacked-on analysis: "...highlighting its lasting influence"
- "Not just X, but Y" constructions
- Reflexive lists of three
- Stock words: delve, intricate, landscape, foster, crucial, vibrant, seamless, robust, leverage, unlock, elevate, journey
- Generic openers and "In conclusion" paragraphs that repeat the text
- Fake balance on every claim, vague "challenges and future outlook" sections
- Long sentences that say little; bold on every key term; emoji in headings
- Uniform paragraph rhythm; heavy em dashes or colon reveals
- Assistant residue: "Certainly!", "Here's a...", "I hope this helps"
Write instead: lead with the point, use concrete detail, strong plain verbs, varied rhythm, one consistent voice. Flag missing evidence as [needs source] instead of inventing it.

## Web and UI
- Build from the subject, not a SaaS template. Pick structure from content (editorial column, index, timeline, catalogue, dashboard).
- Type: choose a headline voice and a text face; at most two families; body 16 to 20px, line height 1.4 to 1.6, 45 to 75 characters per line.
- Colour: roles (background, text, dominant, support, accent). Gradients only when they model something.
- Cards only for independent comparable items. Use one icon set. No invented testimonials or numbers.
- Motion only for feedback or orientation; respect reduced-motion.
- Semantic HTML, one h1, 44px touch targets, works from 320px wide.

## Posters, infographics, charts
- One message, one focal point, a grid, equal margins, scale contrast of about 3x between levels.
- Cut text. Headlines state findings. One illustration style. One or two accent colours.
- Charts: pick type by question, start bars at zero, remove 3D and heavy gridlines, label directly, grey for context and one colour for the point, state units and source, title as takeaway. Never invent or smooth data.
- Follow competition rules and judging criteria first. Show the real prototype or data.

## Slides
- Headline is the claim; body is the evidence. One idea per slide. One master with a few layouts. Body 24pt or larger. No stock handshakes.

## Typography details
Curly quotes, en dashes for ranges, avoid widows, tabular numbers in tables, add letter-spacing to uppercase, left-align body text.

## Scoring (100 points, 10 each)
Originality, Typography, Composition, Hierarchy, Clarity, Authenticity, Consistency, Craftsmanship, Functionality, Context.
Critical failures cap the score at 69: invented facts, unreadable text, broken core function, misleading data, violated constraints, leftover placeholders or assistant text, plagiarism.
90 or more needs written evidence for each dimension and an honest list of remaining weaknesses. Scores are judgments, not measurements.

## Final check before delivering
Facts sourced from the user. Rules and limits met. Works on phone, keyboard, 200% zoom. Contrast passes. Squint test passes. Every default justified. Every decoration has a purpose. Spacing, radii and type consistent. No placeholder or assistant text left.
